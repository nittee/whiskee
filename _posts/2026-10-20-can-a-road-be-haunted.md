Can a road be haunted?

Something that you pass through, and never stay on? Can spirits inhabit such a place? Could memories inhabit such an unhome?

I thought of Balete Drive in Quezon City. Among the countless times I commuted, I would pass by the infamous driveway.

<img src="https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/7926Balete_Drive_Quezon_City_Landmarks_27.jpg/1280px-7926Balete_Drive_Quezon_City_Landmarks_27.jpg" 
width="350">
*Image from Wikimedia*

Named after the big, beautiful -- yet spooky -- balete trees. Thick, veiny branches that rises tall and mighty, like an emperor. But at the same time, possessing the calmness of an elder. It has a canopy that grows up like an explosion, and then arches back like it's giving an embrace. Oftentimes, they would wear hanging roots, giving them an even more magical appearance.

<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3a/Enchanted_Balete_Tree_in_Lazi.JPG" 
width="350">
*Image from Wikimedia*

Most baletes are strangler figs. Their roots capturing an existing tree. Surrounding it, embracing it, suffocating it. Growing around it and eventually consuming it. Like a zombie for trees.

I once read that nature is hundreds of lives and deaths, interacting, culminating in such beautiful greens and browns and yellows and so on...

Can a road be haunted?

A road lined up with big spooky trees must be haunted. I heard stories of creatures, ghosts, spirits. A lady in white. A vengeful woman. A soul seeking justice.

Whenever I commute in the city, I would pass by its most haunted road. Daytime. Nighttime. Hundreds of times I passed it by. Yet all I saw is a road with trees.

Can a road be haunted?

One Friday afternoon, while I was commuting home, the jeepney passed by a familiar street. It's a street I had passed by for countless times. It was near Balete Drive, but it was never haunted...

But on that afternoon, I felt an immense presence on that street. A presence that made my knees weak. A presence that made my stomach drop and chest ache.

On that afternoon, I saw myself running on the brick sidewalks. He was sweating, panting, yet his legs never stopped. As if he was running from something. He was running from something.

A road lined up with trees. I never heard stories in it. But on that day I discovered one. Of a person crushed by grief. A friend abandoned with nowhere to go. A soul seeking justice.

I remember the way the morning sun baked his face and his chest. The way his legs and his core sags and the way his chest constricts. I remember his brain in 4 hours of sleep, replaying their last conversation. The conversation that ended a meaningful friendship. I remember his heart running as if he's the one chasing it. He would run across the city and not get tired. And it will all start... on that street.

Can a road be haunted?

Perhaps every road has stories to tell. Ghosts to hold. Tales of life and death and love and loss intertwined and arching over it like a balete tree.

On that afternoon, I saw a ghost. A ghost of myself.

Can a road be haunted?
